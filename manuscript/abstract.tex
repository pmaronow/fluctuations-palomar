We study the heat-bath (Glauber) dynamics of the Sherrington--Kirkpatrick
model at zero external field and fixed inverse temperature $\beta>1$. We
sample the disorder $J$ and then a configuration $X_0$ from the Gibbs
measure, and run the continuous-time dynamics with rate one per site from
$X_0$. Let $T^J(X_0)$ be the first time at which the law of the configuration,
conditional on $J$ and the realized $X_0$, is within total-variation distance
$1/4$ of the Gibbs measure. We prove lower
and upper bounds: for every fixed $\omega\in(0,1/4)$, with probability tending
to one,
\[
 A_\beta n^{7/30}<\log T^J(X_0)\le n^{1-\omega},
\]
where $A_\beta>0$ depends only on $\beta$. Hence, if
$\log T^J(X_0)=n^{\zeta+o(1)}$, then $7/30\le\zeta\le3/4$; the physics
literature suggests $\zeta=1/3$. Both bounds also hold for the discrete-time
chain.

These results improve on OpenAI's work in the same setting in two ways.
First, OpenAI proved a lower bound with exponent $1/10000$~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{oai-barriers} and an upper bound
with exponent $1-1/40000000$~\cite[Theorem~1]{oai-typical-upper}. We follow
the architecture of both proofs. The lower bound adds a three-replica
comparison whose pressure deficit is cubic in the width of the overlap window,
together with the $n^{-7/10}$ upper bound of Aronow and
Lopatto~\cite[Theorem~1.1]{hexagon-bias} on the mean bias. The upper bound adds a pathwise cancellation between information and
energy, concentration estimates on a growing mesh, and a choice of exponents
that reaches every saving below $1/4$. Second, OpenAI formalized its lower
bound in the Lean~4 proof assistant but not its upper bound; we have
formalized both of our bounds in Lean.

We also formalize two known results that we present. The first is OpenAI's
theorem that the variance of the logarithm of the partition function is
$n^{1/3+o(1)}$. OpenAI's own formalization of this theorem assumes a bound on
positive logarithmic moments that is proved in their paper but not
formalized; our formal proof establishes this bound. The second is the
consequence, recorded by Aronow and Lopatto~\cite[Section~1.6]{hexagon-bias},
that the mean bias of the pressure is at least of order $n^{-5/6-\varepsilon}$ for every fixed
$\varepsilon>0$.
